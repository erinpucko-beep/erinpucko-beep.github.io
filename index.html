Union Made

Detroit, 1940. As a targeted attack on her labor leader husband escalates into a deadly union turf war, Josephine Hoffa refuses to leave her family's future to fate.

When the law cannot—or will not—protect the people she loves, Josephine turns to union organizer Sylvia O'Brien, whose connections to Detroit's underworld may be their only shield.

Behind the pages

About Me

I'm Erin Pucko, an archivist in Salem, Massachusetts, with a master's in library and information science and a habit of getting interested in women who don't quite behave as history expects them to.

I write upmarket historical fiction about women navigating complicated loyalties, dangerous choices, and the gap between what happened and what made it into the historical record.

My debut novel, Union Made, is currently seeking representation. I'm also working on The Queen of Chantilly, a novel about Sophie Dawes.

When I'm not writing, I'm usually somewhere in the archives, following a question I hadn't intended to ask.
